# IEEE Quarterly Projects (QP)

## What is IEEE QP?

IEEE Quarterly Projects is a beginner-friendly hardware hackathon hosted by UCSD's IEEE chapter that is 10-weeks (1 quarter) long.

## What is IEEE?

IEEE is the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. It is the world's largest engineering organization and governing body.

The IEEE club at UCSD is one of the largest student chapters in the United States. IEEE hosts technical workshops, social, and networking events for students to learn more about engineering industries, professional development, and making friends.

## What do we do for QP?

For QP, all people who apply, no matter their previous experience, will be accepted and placed into a team. They will either be placed into a team with their preferred teammates or randomly selected.

## How are Teams Chosen?

Teams will not be "100% randomly selected". Besides teammate preferences, teams are formed based on experience level. Each team should have about 1-2 "experienced" teammates and the rest of the team will have beginners or less experienced students.

## How do we start our project?

Once you meet your teammates and organize your communication platform (discord, texts, etc), you will begin working on your project. Your project should be relevant to that Quarter's theme. For example, the theme for Fall 2026 was "Automotive". Projects do not need to be strictly about popular things from the theme, but it just needs to be relevant in some way shape or form. The first step will be to submit a project proposal with your team.

## What is Project Proposal?

The Project Proposal is a starting point for your team and your project. It should act as a concrete point in your project where you have a concept that has potential to turn into a nicely finished project. You do not need to strictly adhere to your project proposal, but it's there to make sure you don't change your mind midway through the quarter and lose all of your progress. It should also be reasonable for the amount of time you and your teammates have, so you don't try something too time-consuming or expensive.

## How do we work on our Project?

After submitting your project proposal, you and your team will be mostly working independently from the QP management. It will now be your team's responsibility to manage work meetings, research technical information, and make progress.

The QP chairs will host events, like "Workathons" and technical workshops, to help in-person, talk to teams about their progress, and have teams talk to each other about ideas.

There will also be milestone reports to check in with teams and make sure that everything is going smoothly, check what teams need, requesting help, or reporting issues.

There will not be enough QP-hosted events for you and your team to complete your project. Each team will have to work on their own time to complete the project in time for the showcase. QPs will start at around week 2 and end at week 9, so there is about 7 weeks of time to work on your project. Use your time wisely. Plan and work early because teams should not be working while studying for quizzes/midterms/finals. It is each team's decision of whether they want to procrastinate or not. I highly recommend doing work early in the quarter.

## Where can we work on our Project?

You can work on your project remotely with your team, going to a UCSD makerspace, or going to the IEEE project space (4th Floor Jacobs Hall). The IEEE Project Space (PS) has Open Access Hours (OAH) which are posted on the IEEE UCSD website. During OAH, an officer will be at the project space with the door set open for any student to come in to work under supervision. There are soldering stations available, breadboards, wires, resistors, electronic components and Arduino boards.

## What can we use from the Project Space?

Any items from the PS must be requested in a form and returned by the end of the quarter in the same condition, or else you will be charged for the item.

If you want to use any of the soldering stations, you need training from an experienced IEEE officer. Using a soldering station without proper training can result in excessive volatile organic compound (VOC) inhalation and severe burns.

Again, NOBODY can use the 3D printers in the project space. Those are reserved for the RoboCup team.

## What is the Devpost?

At the end of the quarter (around week 9), QP has a showcase for the teams. Before the showcase, there's a Devpost submission which is for submitting a recording of a demo and any code repositories. The Devpost is for a review that your project functions and that a majority of the software was not written by AI. The Devpost will account for about 50% of your grading for the competition standings.

## What is the end-of-quarter Showcase?

The in-person showcase will account for the other 50% of your grading. The showcase will be in a science fair style where each team will get along the walls of a conference room and mentors will go around and judge each project. This is a chance for teams to show off their projects and its features with a live demonstration. If live demos do not work, then we can always judge based off of the Devpost demo. If there is a small number of teams for a given quarter (most likely Spring Quarter), then the showcase will have each team do a presentation to the mentors and rest of the QP teams.

## Who are the Mentors?

Mentors will either be IEEE board members, previous QP participants, or any other student volunteers who can provide help to teams in QP. The mentors can offer advice and information about how to build, program, and design things. If there are enough mentors, then certain teams will have a designated mentor assigned to them. If there are an insufficient number of mentors to assign to each team, then mentors will help any team in general.

## What are the Rules?

For the most part, there are no hard rules to QP.

The general restrictions are that you cannot copy another team's entire project to the point that they are too similar. We want each team to generally have a unique project. Obviously, some teams might have a big overlap in their ideas or designs, which is fine. Just do not copy another team's entire design too much.

Teams are allowed to ask other teams for advice or ask how another team designed a certain part of the project that is common between the two teams. For example, I am programming an Arduino board to blink, but I don't know how to. I am still allowed to go to another team who has a blinking Arduino board and ask how they made it. Try to learn how to do the task rather than just copying the software or designs.

There is no physical size restriction to your project. You can make it the size of a car if you want, and you can make it microscopic too. Make sure to be reasonable with how much time you're willing to spend and your teammates' time too.

There is a budget restriction for each team. Each team gets \$50 of budget from IEEE to spend on hardware and parts. In addition to the budget rule, for each team member in a given team that is an IEEE member, they get an additional \$11 added to your budget (IEEE annual membership fee is about \$32.00 and you get other benefits from the IEEE organization). If your team does not show up to the showcase, then you will not get reimbursed for any of your budget.

You can spend money out-of-pocket, but you will not get reimbursed beyond the \$50 for your team. Please do not spend too much money out-of-pocket. There is no restriction on it, but I do not want someone dumping hundreds of dollars into their project and unfairly competing against other teams who cannot spend too much money. If your team wants to spend lots of money on their project, then I recommend making it a personal project and reaching out to the QP chair for further information.

## Are there rules on AI?

Yes, there are somewhat strict rules on AI. QP is made for students to learn technical skills and knowledge from asking other students and mentors. We don't want people to 100% vibecode the software for their project, and we use AI to design the CAD for their project. You can use AI chatbots for figuring things out or learning how to use tools. For example, I actually recommend using AI to help debug your code or teach you how to program in Arduino or set up your IDE. AI is very useful, and it can save a lot of time. It can also replace the job of the mentors in a lot of cases, but there are still limitations because it takes more time to give ALL of the context of your project into a chatbot. Mentors will already know what you're dealing with and understand how to fix your issue quicker. Mentors also aren't available 24/7, so AI is fine to use too. I am just recommending asking mentors for help because AI can make assumptions and doesn't always teach you specific details about optimizing a project for your purposes.

For the code in the Devpost (if you had any software), I will do a review to make sure that a majority of the code is human-written. If you used a lot of AI on some complicated stuff, then I'll allow it because we only have 10-weeks. I'm not expecting you to start from zero experience and learn how to code a website and handle API calls and HTTP requests.

If you want to program something super complex/complicated that involves a decent amount of hardware design, then I will be more lenient on using AI to help.

If you are unsure about using AI or anything else, then contact the QP chairs for questions and concerns.

## How can I leave QP after joining?

If you joined QP and want/need to leave, then notify your team and the QP chair. You can leave QP at any moment, but it's best to do it as soon as possible if you need to. You may not be reimbursed for any purchases for your team if you leave QP too late in the quarter.
